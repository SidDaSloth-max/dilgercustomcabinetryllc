Commercial Cabinetry Has Different Requirements Than A Kitchen At Home

A break room cabinet, a reception desk, or a retail display unit gets used differently than cabinetry in a private home — more hands, more hours, less patience for a drawer that sticks. Commercial cabinetry needs to survive that use without looking beat up after the first year, which means construction and hardware choices matter even more than they do residentially. We build commercial cabinetry the same careful way we build a home kitchen, just specified for a harder-working environment.

We work with business owners, general contractors, and property managers throughout Bothell and the surrounding area on everything from a single built-in reception desk to a full break room or retail buildout, at any scale a project requires. Because we handle both the build and the install ourselves, we can work around a business’s schedule — installing after hours or during a slower stretch so cabinetry work doesn’t interrupt whatever the space is actually used for.

Built For Daily Use, Not Just Opening Day

Commercial cabinetry from Dilger uses the same full-extension, soft-close drawer hardware and solid construction as our residential work, specified for higher-traffic use — durable laminate or hardwood surfaces, reinforced corners on high-contact pieces, and finishes chosen for how easy they are to clean and maintain over years of daily use, not just how they look on install day.

Working Around Your Business Hours

Downtime is a real cost for most businesses, so commercial installs are scheduled around yours — evenings, weekends, or slower seasons, whatever keeps the disruption to a minimum. We’ll talk through scheduling as part of the initial quote, along with the same on-site measurement and design process we use for residential cabinetry, so there are no surprises once the crew shows up to install.

Accessibility & Code Considerations

Commercial spaces often have accessibility and building code requirements that a home kitchen doesn’t — counter heights, knee clearance under a reception desk, or ADA-compliant reach ranges. We factor those requirements into the design conversation up front rather than as an afterthought, and can work alongside your architect, designer, or property manager if the project already has plans in place.

Materials Chosen For Traffic, Not Just Looks

We’ll walk through material options as part of the quote — laminate and thermofoil surfaces that resist scuffing in high-traffic break rooms, hardwood veneers for a reception area that needs to look upscale, and cabinet-grade plywood boxes throughout so drawers and doors keep working under repeated daily use instead of loosening up after a few months.
