Carpentry Is What Turns A House Into A Home That Fits You

Cabinets get most of the attention, but carpentry is what actually shapes how a room feels day to day: a window seat with real storage underneath instead of dead space, a mudroom bench with cubbies sized for your family’s actual shoes and bags, built-in shelving that fits a specific wall instead of a generic bookcase pushed against it. This is the work that separates a house that’s been decorated from one that’s been genuinely built out.

Every carpentry project starts the same way a cabinet project does — we come look at the space, talk through how you’d actually use a built-in there, and design around real dimensions rather than a standard size. Because we build cabinetry too, our carpentry work shares the same materials and finish quality as our kitchens, so a built-in bench or shelving unit looks like it was part of the house’s original plan rather than added on later.

Carpentry & Millwork We Build

  • Built-in seating: window seats, dining nooks, and mudroom benches, often with storage drawers or lift-tops built in
  • Shelving & built-ins: fireplace-flanking shelving, home office built-ins, and media walls
  • Mudroom & entry millwork: cubbies, coat hooks, and boot benches sized to your entryway
  • Trim carpentry: crown molding, wainscoting, and door and window casing
  • Closet & pantry systems: built-in shelving and drawer systems in place of wire racks

Built-Ins Designed To Match Your Existing Trim

One of the most common carpentry requests we get is matching new work to trim that’s already in the house — a new built-in bookcase that needs to look original to a 1990s Bothell colonial, or a mudroom bench that has to match the baseboard profile already running through a Snohomish farmhouse. We pay attention to those details because a built-in that doesn’t match its surroundings reads as an afterthought, no matter how well it’s built.

Built To Handle Pacific Northwest Humidity

Wood moves with the seasons, and built-ins are more exposed to that than a piece of furniture that can be moved or replaced — a window seat gets direct sun and temperature swings a bookshelf across the room doesn’t. We choose materials, joinery, and finishes with that in mind, so seams stay tight and doors keep closing properly through a full Pacific Northwest year rather than just on install day.

What Carpentry Projects Typically Cost & How Long They Take

Carpentry projects vary more in scope than cabinets do — a single mudroom bench is a much smaller project than a full built-in wall unit with shelving, cabinets, and trim all combined. Most single built-ins take one to two weeks from final design to install; larger, multi-piece projects take longer. As with our cabinet work, every carpentry quote starts with an on-site visit so the number you get reflects your actual space, not a rough guess.
